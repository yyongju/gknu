2022 Revised Informatics National Curriculum in South Korea


Software & Life - High School (Convergence Elective Subject)

English translation of the Ministry of Education Notice No. 2022-33 [Annex 10], Practical Arts (Technology·Home Economics) / Informatics Curriculum. Achievement standard codes appear in the original as [12소생xx-xx]; they are rendered here as [12SL.xx-xx].

1. The Nature and Objectives


A. The Nature of the Subject

'Informatics' is a subject that develops the ability and attitude to recognize the changes in the digital world brought about by data and information in a society defined by artificial intelligence, to explore the social value of information, and to solve problems in real life and in various academic fields on the basis of computational thinking grounded in the diverse principles and technologies of information processing. Responding to national and social demands in the era of digital transformation, 'Informatics' provides the essential competencies that members of society must possess in order to solve problems through computing. The academic foundation of 'Informatics' encompasses the principles of data and information processed by computers, the technologies and methods for designing and implementing computing systems, and an understanding of the human society that deals with information. In other words, on the basis of the basic concepts and principles of informatics — which covers not only computer science but also data science, artificial intelligence, information technology, information systems, and software engineering — 'Informatics' cultivates the knowledge and skills that help solve problems in various academic fields and in future society. These characteristics of the subject help to nurture talented people who possess a basic grounding in the software and artificial intelligence required in every sector of society, and who creatively solve problems not only in engineering but also in diverse academic fields such as the natural sciences, the humanities and social sciences, and the arts and physical education.

'Software & Life' allows students to experience the process of applying the basic concepts and principles of software to problem solving in real life and in various academic fields in a convergent and collaborative manner. By recognizing the value and importance of the convergence of software with each field, and by experiencing expression using software, the analysis and use of data, and the implementation of simulations through software, students grow into digital democratic citizens equipped with the ability to solve problems in real life and in various academic fields in a convergent and creative way. Furthermore, on the basis of experiences of convergence between different fields and of creative problem solving through convergence, students come to grasp for themselves the importance of problem solving using software, and explore career paths in a variety of fields.

B. Objectives

'Software & Life' focuses on recognizing the value and necessity of software, which creates new value by converging with problem solving in real life and in various academic fields in a digital society, and on cultivating the ability to solve the problems at hand effectively and creatively from the perspective of convergence, thereby developing values and attitudes that can contribute to society.

(1) Grasp the value and necessity of the software used in real life and in various academic fields, and develop the ability and attitude to discover problems that can be solved through software.
(2) On the basis of an understanding of methods of expression using software and hardware, develop the ability to select and use the method best suited to conveying an idea or phenomenon effectively.
(3) Cultivate the ability to collect, process, and analyze the data arising in each sector of society in accordance with the purpose, and to interpret their meaning from the perspective of convergence with software.
(4) Recognize the necessity of implementing software in order to solve problems in real life and in various academic fields, and develop the ability to implement and improve programs through simulation.
(5) Explore cases of software start-ups, and develop the ability and attitude to carry out projects that develop software able to contribute to society on the basis of creative ideas.

2. Contents and Achievement Standards


A. Contents of Five Domains

(1) Software That Changes the World
Big Ideas
  • As digital technology advances, software is transforming human life and society as a whole
  • The convergence of academic fields with software helps to investigate and solve the problems and phenomena of the world effectively

Knowledges & Understandings
  • Software and social change
  • Software convergence and problem solving
Process & Practices
  • Explore cases in which software has changed the world
  • Predict future society in the light of the advance of software
  • Explore cases in which problems can be solved through convergence with software
Values & Attitudes
  • A posture of viewing problems and phenomena from the perspective of software
  • A posture of applying software to problem solving in real life and in various academic fields
(2) Software That Supports Creation
Big Ideas
  • Combining various devices and sensors with a work through software supports the creation of the work
  • Creating works through physical computing helps to realize ideas and to solve problems

Knowledges & Understandings
  • Physical computing tools
  • Media art
  • Wearable devices
Process & Practices
  • Analyze the composition and operating principles of physical computing systems
  • Explore sensors and actuators suited to the purpose
  • Implement works through physical computing

  • Diversity and flexibility in the expression of ideas through software
  • Reflection on the value of software used in a variety of fields
(3) Software That Analyzes Phenomena
Big Ideas
  • Software that handles data is utilized to collect, process, and analyze efficiently the vast quantities of data arising in each sector of society
  • Data analysis helps to interpret the phenomena of various fields rationally

Knowledges & Understandings
  • Methods of collection by data type
  • Data visualization and analysis
Process & Practices
  • Explore data from various fields
  • Process and manage data
  • Analyze data and grasp their meaning

  • Recognition of the social value of data
  • An attitude of making ethical use of the results of data analysis
(4) Software That Runs Simulations
Big Ideas

Big Ideas

  • Software start-up projects require an ethical and collaborative process of problem solving

B. Achievement Standards of Five Domains

(1) Software That Changes the World

[12SL.01-01] Explore cases in which software has changed the world, and analyze the influence of software on social change.
[12SL.01-02] View real-world problems and phenomena from the perspective of software, and predict the changes in future society that will accompany the advance of software.
[12SL.01-03] On the basis of cases of problem solving through software convergence, compare and analyze methods of solving problems through convergence with software in various academic fields.

(2) Software That Supports Creation
[12SL.02-01] Analyze the composition and operating principles of works implemented with physical computing tools.
[12SL.02-02] Select the sensors and actuators needed to express an idea through software and construct a physical computing system.
[12SL.02-03] Create a work of media art through physical computing, and grasp the value of the software used in its creation.
[12SL.02-04] Spread the value of software through the process of creating and sharing a wearable device work.

(3) Software That Analyzes Phenomena
[12SL.03-01] Recognize the importance and value of data that can be used to analyze social phenomena, and explore data to devise ways of using them.
[12SL.03-02] Collect data by methods suited to the data type, and process and manage them in accordance with the purpose.
[12SL.03-03] Analyze and visualize data to interpret the meaning of various social phenomena.
[12SL.03-04] On the basis of an understanding of the social value of data, analyze the data and grasp their meaning, and then make ethical use of the results.

(4) Software That Runs Simulations
[12SL.04-01] Understand the concept and components of simulation programs and grasp their value.
[12SL.04-02] Explore the fields in which various simulation programs are used and devise ways of using them.
[12SL.04-03] Express the principles of a complex problem or phenomenon as a simulation model in accordance with the methods of constructing simulation programs.
[12SL.04-04] Implement a simulation model by actively making use of software.

(5) Software That Creates Value
[12SL.05-01] Understand the concept of the software start-up and analyze cases of software start-ups that create new value.
[12SL.05-02] Understand the process of carrying out a software start-up project, and devise a software start-up idea by analyzing users' needs.
[12SL.05-03] Collaboratively design and implement software suited to a start-up project.
[12SL.05-04] Evaluate the value of the software developed from social, functional, and ethical perspectives.

3. Teaching/Learning & Evaluations


A. Teaching and Learning

A.1 Directions of Teaching and Learning

(a) Present learning tasks that require learners to solve problems through computing in the contexts of real life, so that learners naturally cultivate computational thinking, digital culture literacy, and artificial intelligence literacy in the process of solving the tasks on their own.
(b) Construct guidance plans for deep learning that cultivates competencies through learner-customized teaching and learning — for example, by providing opportunities for learners to make choices about learning materials, the learning environment, and the learning process in consideration of learners' interests and diversity, and by increasing opportunities for learner participation in the design of teaching and learning.
(c) Using the knowledge and understanding and the processes and skills of the 'Software & Life' subject, present cross-curricular themes such as sustainable development, together with the various social problems facing the present era such as democratic citizenship education and ecological transition education, as teaching and learning tasks, thereby providing experiences of problem solving with agency.
(d) So that students can adapt to the digital education environment, organize lessons through online-offline linked classes and the use of various digital tools, so as to minimize the cognitive burden of digital tools while increasing experience in using them.
(e) In the teaching and learning of 'Software & Life', organize learning experiences in consideration of the connections between subjects and between the domains within the subject, so that convergent thinking is cultivated; and ensure that convergent problem solving goes beyond fragmentary experience to become meaningful problem solving, so that the transfer of learning into life takes place.

A.2 Methods of Teaching and Learning

(a) In order to cultivate subject competencies, select and utilize teaching and learning methods appropriate for acquiring the big ideas of each domain, such as problem-based learning, project-based learning, design-based learning, pair programming, and inquiry learning. In particular, 'Software & Life' can make active use of project activities for real-life experience and application across all domains.
(b) Take into account that learners may learn at widely differing paces, and endeavour to minimize learning gaps by producing and providing online learning materials through a learning management system (LMS) so that a minimum level of achievement can be guaranteed.
(c) When learners find it difficult to select a topic for project activities, the scope of topic selection may be presented in specifically limited form. Example: instead of presenting a free topic, present a specific topic such as 'protecting the ecosystem'.
(d) Various methods may be used when forming groups for project activities, but before forming the groups, discuss the common themes related to the project and form the groups around those common themes, so that students can become immersed in the activity. Example: if topics such as 'extinction of marine life', 'ocean acidification', and 'marine litter' have been derived for a project on 'protecting the marine ecosystem', have students who are interested in the same topic form a group.
(e) In collaborative project activities, make active use of digital technologies such as shared documents, messengers, and email so that learning processes and outcomes can be shared easily, and guide learners to observe courtesy in language and to communicate in positive terms when interacting with one another.

B. Evaluation

B.1 Directions of Evaluation

(a) Assessment items are specified on the basis of the sub-factors of computational thinking, digital culture literacy, and artificial intelligence literacy.
(b) Assessment content reflects in multiple dimensions not only knowledge and understanding but also the aspects of processes and skills and of values and attitudes, and realizes assessment that helps students grow and develop through process-oriented assessment.
(c) Through the process of constructing specific assessment rubrics together with students, provide opportunities for students to grasp their own level of learning and reflect on their learning for themselves, so that active and self-directed learning takes place.
(d) Various digital tools may be used for efficient assessment, but so that students are not disadvantaged in the assessment by inexperience in using digital tools, minimize the burden of learning how to use the tools or provide sufficient opportunity to become familiar with them.

B.2 Methods of Evaluation

(a) Analyze and reorganize the achievement standards to plan an assessment portfolio that is not limited to written tests and that can contribute to students' growth. For example, utilize observational assessment, descriptive assessment, and performance assessment, or implement multi-faceted assessment such as self-assessment and peer assessment.
(b) Depending on the assessment content and method, various digital tools may be used (automated program assessment systems such as online judges, learning management systems (LMS), and the like); plan to train students to handle the digital tools before the assessment so that they suffer no disadvantage in the assessment.
(c) Prepare specific and objective assessment criteria and checklists so that, in addition to assessing the process and products of group project activities overall, collaboration, presentation, discussion, communication, and collaborative attitudes can be assessed rationally. Use these criteria as tools for self-assessment and peer assessment as well as for teacher assessment.
(d) When assessing discussion and debate, use a discussion record sheet that includes the claims, the grounds, and the conclusions drawn from the discussion, and assess what claim the student sought to make, whether objective grounds were presented, and what conclusion was reached through organizing their thoughts after the debate.
