2022 Revised Informatics National Curriculum in South Korea


Informatics - Middle School

1. The Nature and Objectives


A. The Nature of the Informatics Subject

'Informatics' is a subject that develops the ability and attitude to recognize the changes in the digital world caused by data and information, explore the social value of information, and solve problems in real life and various academic fields based on computational thinking skills based on various principles and technologies for processing information in a society defined by artificial intelligence. 'Informatics' responds to national and social needs in the era of digital transformation and provides essential competencies for members of society to solve problems using computing. The academic foundation of "Informatics" encompasses the principles of data and information processed by computers, technologies and methods for designing and implementing computing systems, and an understanding of human societies that deal with information. In other words, "Informatics" fosters knowledge and skills to help solve problems in various academic fields and future societies based on the basic concepts and principles of computing, which includes not only computer science but also data science, artificial intelligence, information technology, information systems, and software engineering. This characteristic of the course helps to cultivate talented people who have basic skills in software and artificial intelligence required in various fields of society and creatively solve problems not only in engineering but also in various academic fields such as natural sciences, humanities and social sciences, arts and physical education. Informatics, which is the foundation of digital literacy that all students should have, enables students to grow into human beings with the computing and digital competence and self-direction required by the future society. It enables students to become digital citizens with knowledge information processing based on computational thinking, creative thinking, collaborative communication and community capabilities to collaborate and share with others. Middle School "Informatics" aims to cultivate the basic skills and attitudes necessary to solve problems in the future society based on computing and artificial intelligence technologies and understanding of digital culture. Middle School "Informatics" has connections with the digital society and artificial intelligence areas in elementary school and all subjects in high school informatics courses. It also provides a foundation for students to recognize new issues in the field through convergence with other subjects.

B. Objectives

Middle School "Informatics" focuses on developing students' basic ability to discover and solve problems in various fields in the future society by utilizing computing technologies, including artificial intelligence, based on computational thinking.

(1) Understand computing devices that handle data and information in the digital world, and develop the ability to judge the impact of results processed by systems that handle information in real life.
(2) Understand the principles of information processed by computers and develop the ability to collect, analyze, and process data, taking into account the importance of data in helping to interpret the meaning of various phenomena.
(3) Develop an attitude of understanding and practicing the necessity and importance of automation in the process of discovering, analyzing, and abstracting problems to devise solutions and designing and implementing programs to solve real-life problems using computing.
(4) Understand the changes in the world caused by artificial intelligence, and develop the attitude and ability to explore the possibility of solving problems using artificial intelligence based on basic knowledge.
(5) Understand the characteristics of a digital society that deals with information, explore the influence of digital technology in the future society, and develop the attitude to practice digital ethics necessary to live in a digital society.

2. Contents and Achievement Standards


A. Contents of Five Domains

(1) Computing systems
Big Ideas
  • Computing systems that work through the organic connection of hardware and software are utilized to increase social and technological value
  • Designing computing systems helps to understand the overall flow of the system and the value of resource allocation
Categories Content Elements
Knowledges & Understandings
  • How computing systems work
  • Functions of an operating systems
  • Concepts of physical computing
Process & Practices
  • Identify the components of a computing systems and relate how they work to the operating systems
  • Investigate real-world applications of physical computing
  • Configure a physical computing systems
Values & Attitudes
  • Determine the need for and value of computing systems
  • Flexibility in selecting the components of a physical computing system to fit its purpose
(2) Data
Big Ideas
  • Data management involves converting analog data into a digital form that can be processed by a computer
  • To solve a problem, it is necessary to collect the necessary data, analyze it, and interpret its meaning
  • Identifying and structuring relationships between collected data helps to find new knowledge through data
Categories Content Elements
Knowledges & Understandings
  • How to Represent Digital Data
  • Data collection and management
  • Structuring data and interpreting its meaning
Process & Practices
  • Represent diverse data as digital data
  • Collect, categorize, and store data for a purpose
  • Structure and interpret data for meaning
Values & Attitudes
  • Recognize the positive aspects of many real-world data being converted to digital form and utilized
  • Gain a data-driven perspective on phenomena
(3) Algorithm & Programming
Big Ideas
  • Algorithms are utilized to solve everyday problems through various design strategies
  • Algorithms designed for automation are implemented in a language that computers can understand and use to make life easier
  • Program development requires collaboration, and a culture of sharing leads to better programs
Categories Content Elements
Knowledges & Understandings
  • Problem abstraction
  • How to represent algorithms
  • Sequential data storage-list, array
  • Logical operations
  • Nested control structures
  • Functions and Debugging
Process & Practices
  • Define the initial state, current state, and goal state of a problem and structure it into a solvable form
  • Design and apply various algorithms to solve problems
  • Write programs using logical operations, nested control structures, and sequential data storage
  • Modularize programs using functions, and find and correct errors in programs
Values & Attitudes
  • Understand the importance of abstraction through problem analysis and practice solving real-world problems
  • Ability to explore various solutions to problems and express them clearly as algorithms
  • The value of collaboration and sharing through software
  • A willingness to analyze the effectiveness of a program and troubleshoot errors in a program
(4) Artificial Intelligence
Big Ideas
  • Agents enabled by AI technologies are utilized to solve complex and difficult problems that were previously unsolvable through interaction with the outside world
  • Since AI enables problem-solving based on data, it is important to ensure that the data used for AI is free of ethical bias
Categories Content Elements
Knowledges & Understandings
  • Concept and Characteristics of AI
  • AI Systems
Process & Practices
  • ⋅Differentiate between AI software and ordinary software
  • Collect and utilize data for AI training
  • Identify problems that can be solved using AI systems
  • Select an AI system to solve a problem
Values & Attitudes
  • ⋅Identify applicable problems for AI systems
  • Minimize the possibility of problems caused by data in AI learning
(5) Digital Culture
Big Ideas
  • With the development of digital technology come new rules to follow and risks to watch out for in a digital society
  • It is important to understand the relationship between technology and social change, as jobs and careers in the digital world change with the development of technology
Categories Content Elements
Knowledges & Understandings
  • Digital Society and Work
  • Digital Ethics
  • Privacy and Copyright
Process & Practices
  • Exploring how professions are changing due to the nature of the digital society
  • Discuss ethics in digital spaces
  • Explore ways to protect yourself and others in digital spaces
Values & Attitudes
  • Explore how the shift to a digital society affects your life and career decisions
  • Have ethical attitudes about living together in digital spaces


B. Achievement Standards of Five Domains

(1) Computing Systems

[9Inf.01-01] Understand the components and operating principles of computing systems and analyze the functions of operating systems.
[9Inf.01-02] Understand the concept of physical computing and determine the need and value of computing systems through case studies applied in daily life.
[9Inf.01-03] Design a system by selecting physical computing components for problem-solving purposes.

(2) Data
[9Inf.02-01] Explore the positive value of utilizing real-life data converted into digital form and express various data in digital form.
[9Inf.02-02] Collect data suitable for problem solving and categorize and manage it according to the purpose.
[9Inf.02-03] Structure real-life data in various forms such as tables and diagrams.
[9Inf.02-04] Identify the relationship between data based on cases and interpret the meaning based on data.
[9Inf.02-05] Collect and analyze data based on cases from various disciplines to solve problems in an integrated manner.

(3) Algorithm & Programming
[9Inf.03-01] Define the state of a problem and structure it into an actionable form.
[9Inf.03-02] Understand the importance of abstraction for problem solving and express algorithms in terms of key elements.
[9Inf.03-03] Understand the importance of algorithms and compare and analyze various algorithms to solve problems.
[9Inf.03-04] Design algorithms by selecting appropriate strategies to solve problems based on examples.
[9Inf.03-05] Write a problem-solving program using a structure that allows data to be stored sequentially.
[9Inf.03-06] Write programs to solve problems using logical operations and nested control structures.
[9Inf.03-07] Use functions in writing programs and analyze the results of program execution with a debugger to correct errors.
[9Inf.03-08] Explore and discover real-life problems and solve them through programming.
[9Inf.03-09] collaborate to develop software to solve interdisciplinary problems.

(4) Artificail Intelligence
[9Inf.04-01] Explain the concepts and characteristics of artificial intelligence and distinguish artificial intelligence software.
[9Inf.04-02] Understand the importance of data in AI learning, and collect and categorize data required for learning.
[9Inf.04-03] Organize and apply artificial intelligence systems by utilizing various data.
[9Inf.04-04] Identify a problem that can be solved by an AI system and apply an AI system suitable for solving the problem.
[9Inf.04-05] Devise solutions to ethical issues arising from the collection and utilization of data required for AI learning.

(5) Digital Culture
[9Inf.05-01] Explore the characteristics of a digital society and explore the changes in occupations as society changes.
[9Inf.05-02] Democratically discuss rules for living conveniently and safely as a member of a digital society and establish action plans.
[9Inf.05-03] Explore practices to protect privacy, rights, and copyrights for living together in digital spaces through case studies.

3. Teaching/Learning & Evaluations


A. Teaching and Learning

A.1 Directions of Teaching and Learning

(a) Provide learning tasks that require learners to solve problems through computing in real-life contexts, so that learners can naturally cultivate computational thinking, digital culture literacy, and artificial intelligence literacy in the process of solving tasks on their own.
(b) Organize in-depth learning guidance to cultivate competencies through learner-customized teaching and learning, including providing opportunities for learners to make choices about learning materials, learning environments, and learning processes, taking into account learners' interests and diversity, and promoting opportunities for learner participation in the design process of teaching and learning. For example, explore the digital competencies required for teaching and learning in each area and identify students' digital competency levels to provide additional training opportunities to facilitate teaching and learning.
(c) Utilize the knowledge, understanding, processes, and skills of informatics subjects to provide students with proactive problem-solving experiences by presenting cross-curricular topics such as sustainable development as teaching and learning tasks, as well as various social issues facing the current era, such as civic education and ecological transition education.
(d) Expand related competencies through integration with areas within the "informatics" subject, other subjects, and non-curricular activities, and ensure that students' competencies are transferable to various fields.
(e) The order of arrangement of content areas does not necessarily imply the order of teaching and learning, so when developing a teaching and learning plan or preparing an assessment, the content or sequence may be reorganized to take into account the context of the problems to be presented to students, the difficulty of the problems, the readiness of the students, the learning environment, etc.
(f) You may utilize performance standards at lower grade levels and higher grade levels as appropriate, taking into account students' prior knowledge and overall task progression.

A.2 Methods of Teaching and Learning

(a) Select and utilize appropriate teaching and learning methods to acquire core ideas in each area, such as problem-based learning, project-based learning, design-based learning, pair programming, and exploratory learning, in order to develop curricular competencies.
(b) To adapt to the digital education environment, activities are organized to minimize the cognitive burden of digital tools and increase the experience of using them, such as through online and offline linked classes and the use of various digital tools.
(c) Utilize online classrooms, various community services, etc. to provide teaching and learning so that students do not experience learning deficits even if they are not in the classroom.
(d) Organize teaching and learning to include problem-solving through programming in each content area so that students have ample experience applying computing systems to solve problems.
(e) Organize teaching and learning in small groups that take into account individual differences within the classroom to effectively meet learning objectives.

B. Evaluation

B.1 Directions of Evaluation

(a) Assessment items will be refined based on the sub-factors of computational thinking, digital culture literacy, and artificial intelligence literacy.
(b) The assessment content reflects not only knowledge and understanding, but also aspects of process, skills, values, and attitudes, and realizes an assessment that emphasizes process and helps students grow and develop.
(c) Through the process of constructing specific assessment rubrics with students, we provide opportunities for students to identify their learning level and reflect on their learning, so that active learning can take place.
(d) Develop a plan to integrate observation and assessment of problem-solving processes rather than simple, superficial assessments of knowledge.

B.2 Methods of Evaluation

(a) Analyze and restructure performance standards to plan a portfolio of assessments that contribute to student growth and are not limited to written assessments. For example, utilize observational, narrative, and performance assessments, or implement multi-faceted assessments such as self-assessment, peer assessment, etc.
(b) Plan to utilize a variety of digital tools (e.g., program automated assessment systems, learning management systems (LMS), etc.) depending on the assessment content and methodology, and to train students to use digital tools prior to the assessment so that they are not disadvantaged by the assessment.
(c) Conduct quantitative assessments for conceptual or functional clarity, and qualitative assessments for the quality or aesthetics of outputs.
