2022 Revised Informatics National Curriculum in South Korea


Data Science - High School (Career Elective Subject)

English translation of the Ministry of Education Notice No. 2022-33 [Annex 10], Practical Arts (Technology·Home Economics) / Informatics Curriculum. Achievement standard codes appear in the original as [12데과xx-xx]; they are rendered here as [12DS.xx-xx].

1. The Nature and Objectives


A. The Nature of the Subject

'Informatics' is a subject that develops the ability and attitude to recognize the changes in the digital world brought about by data and information in a society defined by artificial intelligence, to explore the social value of information, and to solve problems in real life and in various academic fields on the basis of computational thinking grounded in the diverse principles and technologies of information processing. Responding to national and social demands in the era of digital transformation, 'Informatics' provides the essential competencies that members of society must possess in order to solve problems through computing. The academic foundation of 'Informatics' encompasses the principles of data and information processed by computers, the technologies and methods for designing and implementing computing systems, and an understanding of the human society that deals with information. In other words, on the basis of the basic concepts and principles of informatics — which covers not only computer science but also data science, artificial intelligence, information technology, information systems, and software engineering — 'Informatics' cultivates the knowledge and skills that help solve problems in various academic fields and in future society. These characteristics of the subject help to nurture talented people who possess a basic grounding in the software and artificial intelligence required in every sector of society, and who creatively solve problems not only in engineering but also in diverse academic fields such as the natural sciences, the humanities and social sciences, and the arts and physical education.

'Data Science', on the basis of diverse methodologies for data and data processing, makes use of statistics and machine learning to provide insight in problem solving and decision making across various academic fields. Data science is connected to individual lives, enabling students to respond actively to technological advances in everyday life and in their future occupations. By understanding the fundamental principles of data science and cultivating data literacy, students become able to critically analyze rapidly increasing volumes of data, and grow into digital democratic citizens with insight into future society and environmental change and with responsible self-direction. 'Data Science' cultivates the abilities and attitudes needed to creatively solve problems in real life and in various fields on the basis of computational thinking, and provides foundational experience connected to career paths for which knowledge of data science is required.

B. Objectives

'Data Science' focuses on recognizing, on the basis of computational thinking, the role and potential value of data in a digital society and the importance of the process of problem solving grounded in data science, and on developing the competency of insight needed to solve problems in various fields and to make rational decisions.

(1) Understand the characteristics of society and the value of data as data science advances, and develop the attitude of practicing rational, data-based decision making.
(2) Understand effective methods related to data analysis, and develop the ability to grasp the relationships among data according to the problem situation and to apply various methods of analysis.
(3) Develop the ability and attitude to construct models for solving problems rationally, to compare the various issues that may arise in the problem-solving process, and to find and critically interpret the meaning of the analyzed results.
(4) Recognize that problem solving grounded in data science is effective for rational decision making, and develop the ability and attitude to solve problems by the methods of data science.

B. Achievement Standards of Four Domains

(1) Understanding Data Science

[12DS.01-01] Understand the concept of data science, and apply cases of problem solving to situations of data-based decision making.
[12DS.01-02] Distinguish structured from unstructured data, and grasp the potential value of data in their attributes.
[12DS.01-03] Understand the database as a collection of datasets, and grasp the meaning of integrating data from different datasets into a form that can be analyzed.
[12DS.01-04] Recognize the social changes brought about by data, and analyze cases of data-based problem solving related to careers and occupations.

(2) Data Preparation and Analysis
[12DS.02-01] Collect data so that they are not biased, and analyze the characteristics of the collected data.
[12DS.02-02] Minimize the possibility of error by pre-processing through the detection of outliers and missing values and through normalization, and visualize the data for analysis.
[12DS.02-03] Grasp and integrate the relationships among data attributes in order to analyze the data.
[12DS.02-04] Apply different methods of analysis to the same data and compare the results.

(3) Data Modelling and Evaluation
[12DS.03-01] Understand the concept of the data model and explore tools that can be used for data analysis.
[12DS.03-02] Analyze the same data with a statistical regression model and with a regression model based on machine learning, and compare the interpretations of the results.
[12DS.03-03] Measure and analyze the similarity of data attributes to form clusters, and interpret the meaning of the results of cluster analysis.
[12DS.03-04] Analyze the relationships among data, grasp their mutual associations, and interpret the meaning of the results.
[12DS.03-05] Compare and evaluate the analysis results of data models according to the method of data analysis.
[12DS.03-06] Compare and evaluate various methods of analysis and apply the method most suited to the purpose of the analysis.

(4) Data Science Projects
[12DS.04-01] Investigate and analyze cases in which data science is applied by field, find topics that can be addressed with data, and judge their suitability.
[12DS.04-02] Analyze the collected data exploratively to grasp the meaning within the data, and devise creative methods for solving the problem.
[12DS.04-03] When conducting data analysis, analyze using two or more methods and compare the results.
[12DS.04-04] Adopt the posture of solving even complex and difficult problems through to the end, carry out the analysis, and interpret the meaning of the analysis results.
[12DS.04-05] Reflect on the whole process from the purpose of the analysis through data collection and analysis, and explore ways of utilizing the analysis results in consideration of their social impact.

3. Teaching/Learning & Evaluations


A. Teaching and Learning

A.1 Directions of Teaching and Learning

(a) Present learning tasks that require learners to solve problems through computing in the contexts of real life, so that learners naturally cultivate computational thinking, digital culture literacy, and artificial intelligence literacy in the process of solving the tasks on their own.
(b) Construct guidance plans for deep learning that cultivates competencies through learner-customized teaching and learning — for example, by providing opportunities for learners to make choices about learning materials, the learning environment, and the learning process in consideration of learners' interests and diversity, and by increasing opportunities for learner participation in the design of teaching and learning.
(c) Using the knowledge and understanding and the processes and skills of the Data Science subject, present cross-curricular themes such as sustainable development, together with the various social problems facing the present era such as democratic citizenship education and ecological transition education, as teaching and learning tasks, thereby providing experiences of problem solving with agency.
(d) So that students can adapt to the digital education environment, organize lessons through online-offline linked classes and the use of various digital tools, so as to minimize the cognitive burden of digital tools while increasing experience in using them.
(e) Design teaching and learning so that, through an understanding of data science, students cultivate the attitude and ability to respond actively to data-based social change in a digital society.
(f) Project-type practical work should cultivate communication ability, collaborative problem-solving ability, and recognition of the value of sharing through collaboration.
(g) Take care not to concentrate on acquiring the usage of particular data science technologies or tools; place the focus instead on cultivating data literacy and artificial intelligence literacy through the use of data science technologies for problem solving and through the design and execution of projects.

A.2 Methods of Teaching and Learning

(a) In order to cultivate subject competencies, select and utilize teaching and learning methods appropriate for acquiring the big ideas of each domain, such as problem-based learning, project-based learning, design-based learning, pair programming, and inquiry learning.
(b) Take into account that learners may learn at widely differing paces, and endeavour to minimize learning gaps by producing and providing online learning materials through a learning management system (LMS) so that a minimum level of achievement can be guaranteed.
(c) Include, in the process of collecting and analyzing data, a process in which students judge social impacts such as data bias and ethical issues through discussion and debate and make decisions accordingly. In the data modelling process, guide students, through discussion and debate, to compare and select various methods of data analysis and to interpret the results with a critical eye.
(d) Have learners choose projects on topics connected to their career paths, so that lessons are organized in a way that allows learners to think about the use of data science technologies in connection with their own futures.
(e) Various digital tools needed for collaboration may be used in the course of project activities; introduce digital tools that students can use easily, so as to minimize the cognitive burden of learning how to use digital tools in remote lessons or collaborative activities.

B. Evaluation

B.1 Directions of Evaluation

(a) Assessment items are specified on the basis of the sub-factors of computational thinking, digital culture literacy, and artificial intelligence literacy.
(b) Assessment content reflects in multiple dimensions not only knowledge and understanding but also the aspects of processes and skills and of values and attitudes, and realizes assessment that helps students grow and develop through process-oriented assessment.
(c) Through the process of constructing specific assessment rubrics together with students, provide opportunities for students to grasp their own level of learning and reflect on their learning for themselves, so that active and self-directed learning takes place.
(d) Assess with a focus on the accuracy and efficiency of the programs written, together with the logical soundness of the program design process and whether students understand the process of data modelling through the practical work.
(e) Prepare assessment criteria and specific checklists so that rational and objective assessment can be carried out not only of the products of group inquiry activities but throughout the whole process of collaboration, presentation, and discussion, and use these as tools for self-assessment and peer assessment as well as for teacher assessment.

B.2 Methods of Evaluation

(a) Analyze and reorganize the achievement standards to plan an assessment portfolio that is not limited to written tests and that can contribute to students' growth. For example, utilize observational assessment, descriptive assessment, and performance assessment, or implement multi-faceted assessment such as self-assessment and peer assessment.
(b) Depending on the assessment content and method, various digital tools may be used (automated program assessment systems such as online judges, learning management systems (LMS), and the like); plan to train students to handle the digital tools before the assessment so that they suffer no disadvantage in the assessment.
(c) When assessing practical tasks, assess students' learning processes comprehensively by observing the task-solving process continuously as well as assessing the products. In particular, when project-type tasks are carried out, have learners accumulate their performance processes online and offline so that the overall process is assessed comprehensively.
(d) When assessing collaborative projects, record each learner's role specifically and have students discuss and present the weight of each member's activity through peer assessment, thereby securing fairness as far as possible.
(e) Various digital tools may be used for efficient assessment, but so that students are not disadvantaged in the assessment by inexperience in using digital tools, minimize the burden of learning how to use the tools or provide sufficient opportunity to become familiar with them.
